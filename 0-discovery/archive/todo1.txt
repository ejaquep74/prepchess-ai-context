PEND: Clasificacion general de payrones tacticos y estrategicos. El método híbrido para la clasificación de patrones en ajedrez fusiona la precisión matemática de la programación clásica con la capacidad de abstracción de la inteligencia artificial. En lugar de depender exclusivamente de reglas manuales y etiquetas predefinidas, este enfoque busca descubrir la "esencia" geométrica y táctica de una posición. El objetivo es identificar tanto los motivos teóricos ya conocidos como aquellos patrones ocultos que los motores evalúan positivamente pero que la mente humana aún no ha categorizado, permitiendo agrupar posiciones por su similitud estructural profunda.
El proceso funciona en tres grandes etapas conceptuales. Primero, se aplica un filtro geométrico estricto que "limpia" el tablero, eliminando las piezas irrelevantes para dejar únicamente la estructura o tensión que se desea analizar. Segundo, esta posición purificada se introduce en una red neuronal para extraer su representación vectorial, traduciendo la tensión posicional a coordenadas matemáticas multidimensionales. Finalmente, mediante algoritmos de agrupamiento espacial, el sistema evalúa la distancia entre estos vectores, agrupando automáticamente las posiciones que comparten el mismo ADN táctico.
Para implementar esta arquitectura, existen diversas herramientas y proyectos de referencia clave. En la etapa de filtrado espacial y manejo de bitboards, se utilizan librerías base como python-chess o ChessLib. Para la vectorización y extracción de características mediante inteligencia artificial, los proyectos de referencia son ChessLM y el Stockfish NNUE Extractor jdart1/nnue. Por último, para indexar y agrupar los vectores generados a gran escala, se emplean motores de búsqueda vectorial especializados como FAISS o Milvus.

Yes, there are a few highly specific open-source projects that have already cracked the code on extracting strategic heuristics (pawn structures, outposts, piece activity) directly from PGNs and move trees.
Because you are likely looking for backend parsing logic (especially in Python, which is the dominant language for these specific repos), you don't need to write the extraction algorithms from scratch. You can look at the source code of these projects to see exactly how they mathematically define and extract these concepts.
Here are the best open-source projects right now that take a PGN/FEN and output strategic insights:
Look for *Chessbeast* at github, it uses stockfish and maia to explain lines of moves
1. For Pure Positional Heuristics (Outposts & Structures)
Project: codex-0915/chesslens (GitHub)
What it does: It takes a PGN or Lichess username and runs a multi-dimensional analysis on the games. It specifically includes a "Positional & Strategy" module that explicitly extracts pawn structures, outposts, and piece activity.
How to leverage it: While this is built as a zero-dependency browser app, you can strip its evaluation logic. Look at how it scans a position to tag a square as a true "outpost" or mathematically calculates which piece is the most "active." You can port this exact math into your Scala backend to generate your "Ghost Pieces" or "Target Squares."
2. For AI "Commentator" Explanations
Project: alipala/multiagent_llm_chess (GitHub)
What it does: A Python-based project that uses LangChain and a custom multi-agent system. It specifically has a "Commentator Agent" and a "Black Player Agent" whose sole focus is solid pawn structure and strategic piece placement.
How to leverage it: This project solves the LLM pipeline for you. If you want to feed your move tree into an AI and get back text like "Push this pawn to challenge the center," look at their RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) implementation. They show you exactly how to feed the engine's positional assessment into the LLM prompt to get strategic text back.
3. For Mathematical Move Explanations (Explainable AI)
Project: fspinna/chessplainer (GitHub)
What it does: A brilliant Python package that uses SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) on top of Stockfish.
Why it's useful: Instead of just saying a move is +1.5, it calculates exactly which pieces or squares contributed to that score. If you want to implement the "Find My Worst Piece" feature we discussed earlier, this repository has the exact math to do it. It proves mathematically which piece on the board is dragging the evaluation down the most.
4. For Opening & Pawn Structure Dossiers
Project: gaelazzo/python_chess (GitHub)
What it does: A Python-based chess trainer that analyzes your PGNs. What makes it unique is that it specifically cross-references positions to generate an "opening ideas dossier for the current pawn structure".
How to leverage it: It maps opening moves not just by variations, but by the resulting pawn skeleton. You can look at their parsing logic to see how they group different transpositions into a single strategic "pawn structure" concept.
How to integrate this into your workflow:
If you are running a JVM (Java/Scala) backend with Python scripts handling the data science/parsing side, I would recommend cloning chessplainer and python_chess. You can use Python to chew through your PGN move trees, extract the outposts and worst pieces, and package them into a clean JSON payload. Your main application can then just read that JSON to draw the arrows and highlight the red squares on the board.
